# Local decisions in dated campaigns

The 493, 568 and 634 packs each contain ten local household events, with inclusive
windows 493–513, 568–588 and 634–654 in the late antique era. Each pack opts into
`bookmarkIds` with its own campaign bookmark. The 711 and 800 packs use the same
selector without changing their events. A campaign reaching another opening's
year does not acquire that opening's pack, even after complete catalogue preparation.

Each source registers through `bookmarkSources.eventPack` and retains its literal
event order. Loading a different date alone does not install these declarations;
complete preparation retains the canonical source order and campaign selectors.

The 493 scenes concern private records, tools, devotion and neighbors around
Ravenna, Rome, Ticinum and Soissons. The 568 scenes add local work and accounts
around existing Italian, Frankish and eastern courts. The 634 scenes concern
households at Damascus, Ctesiphon, Medina and the Hira district. These fictional
incidents describe local choices, without asserting a conquest, treaty or assessment.
The packs use existing starts and add no historical identities.

Every event reads the actual county holder's government. County selectors narrow
the audience; mixed-faith scenes also require actual local communities. Neighbor
decisions bind an existing local person and retain their portrait. Purchases
retain resource and duplicate-property gates and an ordinary refusal. Named
Stewardship chances use the same declared formula for preview and resolution.
No scene changes a border, community, office or historical outcome.

The Ravenna repairs and Medinan accounts scenes additionally offer an optional
canonical review when their existing household story offers its supplies choice.
The review charges the existing private expense, respectively three and two,
through `story_choice`; it attaches no story and supplies no public authority.
The ordinary repairs choice and refusal remain available. Canonical blockers,
stale quotes, automation and cancellation use the shared event review foundation.
Cancel, Back and Escape preserve the mounted event and its view state.

## Campaign period integration

The shared foundation owns typed event/pack `bookmarkIds`, override semantics,
canonical saved `state.start.id` and revision checks, event routes and transient
pool invalidation. The general world period reader also honors opted-in identity,
so direct period reads agree with event delivery. Missing, unknown or unavailable
saved identity fails closed; selected title dates never substitute for it.
Unscoped periods retain their era and live-year rules. System gates remain live.
The transient year-pool key retains the revision's type, distinguishing an absent
baseline stamp from an invalid explicit null revision.

Native selector validation recognizes declared bookmarks retained by the source
owner even when their worlds are withheld. This reference check uses the source
owner's authenticated native context; it does not publish a world or enable its
events or starts. Detached ordinary JSON candidates remain strict about their own
bookmark table, including when they carry a copied reference field.

Ordinary mod validation invokes the shared pack validator for every effective
pack, including empty packs and metadata hidden by an event override. Composition
already delegates to that detached candidate path; no new composition or loading
mechanism is needed. No eligibility cache, quote or new envelope is saved.

Semantic fixtures cover every scene's state, county, year boundaries and other
campaign identities. The separate isolation regression covers complete catalogue
preparation, every declared competing bookmark at the same live year, cache
switches, all delivery paths, pure malformed-candidate rejection and two exact
current-content restores. A real JSON import supplies an independent expense,
multi-bookmark pack, event override and legacy unscoped period.

These are configurations and corrections of existing capabilities. Technology
impact remains **none** under `state_aware_events` and `event_canonical_reviews`;
there is no additional technology dependency or save schema.
