# Spring 451 western provinces

The hidden 451 world uses independent regional literals. Its western region
contains 173 counties, including the inherited British islands and middle Rhine.
The physical rows, settlement identities, development and civic institutions
remain in place. Corrections describe the opening, not scheduled conquest,
conversion, displacement or confiscation. Existing static regional display owners
provide translated labels with English fallback; person names remain proper names.

## Courts and provincial ownership

The western imperial court (`italy`, anchored at Ravenna) owns the former
Soissons-domain rows. There is no separate Syagrius or Aetius kingdom. The same
public owner holds Arles, Marseille, Narbonne, Beziers, Clermont and Le Puy, and
the former Burgundian territorial block. This is a conservative provincial
representation, not an exact surviving tax register. Hydatius, *Chronicon*, the
Toulouse peace sequence and subsequent Gothic campaigns, supports separating
Roman provincial authority from later Gothic conquests.

`toulouse_451` is a settled `regnum` at Toulouse under Theodoric I, with only
Thorismund and Theodoric as authored sons. Hydatius, Theodosius XV and XXVIII,
supports the ruler and court; Jordanes, *Getica* 190, supports these two sons.
Their birth years (391, 421 and 425) are approximate gameplay ages. The inherited
Euric-code legal identity is replaced by the existing broad late-Roman legal
identity, an abstraction for mixed settled jurisdiction rather than a dated royal
code. No Roman command over this kingdom or active service agreement is seeded.

Iberian Gothic ownership and settlement minorities from the later scaffold are
removed. Rechiar's Suevic court has a Nicene governing identity and a generated
household rather than an invented named spouse. Hydatius, Theodosius XXIV-XXV,
supports his accession and Christian confession. Lugo remains a coarse Gallaecian
court anchor, not an attested spring residence; his birth year 421 is approximate.
The southern owner corrections use Hydatius's Rechila entries for Emerita,
Hispalis, Baetica and Carthaginiensis. The remaining formerly Gothic Iberian rows
use the western provincial owner, including Tarraconensis; Hydatius's later
Tarraconensian invasion sequence distinguishes it from Suevic territory.

Resident Hispano-Roman and Basque identities remain separate from ruling identity.
Former Gothic shares return to the existing resident majority. The four inherited
Gallaecian mixtures retain their shares but use traditional practice for the
non-Nicene Suevic component: Hydatius distinguishes the pagan predecessor from
his Christian son. This is a coarse mixed-community reconstruction, not evidence
for those exact percentages or a general conversion. Later Homoian institutions
at Lugo, Toledo and Tarragona are omitted; existing civic and Nicene seats remain.

## Separate settlement authorities

`burgundian_settlement_451` is a countyless `federate_polity` anchored at Geneva,
with a generated adult principal. The Gallic Chronicle of 452, entry 128, supplies
the Sapaudian settlement among existing residents. Geneva, Chambery and Grenoble
retain the scaffold's mixed communities; other former Burgundian rows retain
their Roman residents without projecting the later territorial kingdom backward.
Neither these three cells nor their percentages define an exact allotment.

`alan_orleans_451` is a separate countyless `federate_polity` anchored at Orleans.
The city stays under western civic ownership with its existing Roman community
and council. Jordanes, *Getica* 194-197, supplies the Orleans association; the
Gallic Chronicle, entries 124 and 127, distinguishes settlement groups without
making them one territorial Alan state. The principal is generated: Sangiban's
identity is historically supported, but his personal faith remains unresolved.
Nicene is the declaration's local institutional convention, not his confession.
No Alan population percentage, army, betrayal or surrender commitment is seeded.
Neither settlement declaration fabricates a service or land-grant receipt.

## Retained uncertainties

The Burgundian incumbent, Sangiban's personal faith and named binding, exact
settlement boundaries, allotment shares and service terms remain unresolved.
The Burgundian Homoian court convention is inherited, not personal evidence for
an unknown incumbent. Earlier Valence-area Alans are not assigned to Orleans.

The Frankish, Armorican, British, northern Iberian and middle-Rhine blocks retain
coarse boundaries and institutional anchors. Their generated adults assert no
historical dynasty. Childeric, Basina, Clovis and the later Burgundian dynasty
are absent. The Gallic Chronicle's British entry 126 supports upheaval and Saxon
presence, not the exact retained map or a named British ruler. The British and
Frankish faith/community mixtures remain qualified scaffold reconstructions.
Mallorca's inherited owner remains a disputed island hold. Precise Iberian
frontiers, particularly the eastern and northern limits of Suevic authority,
remain coarse; raids are not treated as durable annexations.

These are opening-data corrections using existing capabilities. They add no
technology eligibility, save schema or new mechanic. The bookmark remains hidden.
The western fixture pins owners, courts, faiths, communities, institutions and
the absence of later named households alongside canonical bookmark validation.
