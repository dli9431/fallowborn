# Imperial service stories and court events

The existing 476 court-service household at Salona can petition for an actual
available court-service host, review its exact command and accept a real ended
mandate. The three optional goals require those committed transactions. Fees,
qualifications, court Standing, tenure, custody, succession and host availability
remain with the office owners. There is no opening commission or free host.

Each of the eight household reviews permits a costless conclusion. Gate service,
public accounts, independent civil and religious interests, favor and the purple
remain separate questions. A personal priority supplies no consent or payment.
The loss review waits for actual court dismissal or expiry; suspended Standing
alone does not complete it. Accepted public obligations remain with the court,
and the household keeps its own property and purse.

An optional ordinary paid-service journey from Salona leads to the existing
eastern court. Arrival and the normal year of residence precede the ordinary
one-time household settlement review. A move grants no commission. The eastern
branch then reviews available commissioned forces through the same owners.
Travel, residence and petitions can be interrupted or refused.

In 493 the existing farming household at Constantinople has its own service
track and transaction goals. It must earn the required skills and prestige and
wait for a real available host. No new historical officer identity is invented.
The story source is registered for both dates at its existing script position;
other sources and campaign visibility remain unchanged.

The commander's personal agenda follows the actual non-sovereign holder, not an
independent neighboring ruler. It waits for acquisition and then pins the original
appointment and court generation. Nepos's existing ruler beginning has a court
agenda that can review dismissal of its actual subordinate. The 493 court agenda
waits until the original protagonist actually becomes the eastern court's ruler
through the ordinary political owners; attachment alone gives it no decisions.
Once bound, death, succession or jurisdiction loss interrupts the program.
Legacy ruler agendas keep their original incumbent rules.

## Typed adapters

`household_track` supports `operation:{kind:'mandate_service',service:{...}}`.
`ruler_agenda` supports `operation:'agenda_mandate'` with the same `service`.
Service terms contain `realmId`, `officeId`, `action` and `input`. Supported actions
are `petition`, `command`, `consent`, `confirm`, `accept_loss`, `dismiss`, `acclaim`
and `renounce`. A petition may select `input.hostId`; command may select
`input.destination`. Otherwise the review selects the first actually available
host for a petition and the commissioned host's current ground for an order.
`consent` requires `input.templateId` and selects an outstanding offer already
accepted by its independent outside court. It completes actual bilateral service
through the mandate owner, disclosing initial public payment and one season of
reserve. A proposal or absent outside consent cannot advance this operation.
Its decision provenance additionally retains the canonical `contractId`.
All other inputs are empty. Unknown fields, actions and incompatible offices
reject. Story terms must resolve their court in the attached world.

The mandate owner supplies detached canonical quotes and rechecks the complete
quote before commit. Roman petitions retain their private registration fee;
public commissions retain their productive-source and public-payment rules.
Disabled office systems reject both the adapters and direct mandate transactions.
No adapter creates another authority, consent, contract or account store.
The event review `mandate_service` uses the same typed terms. Its optional
`realmId` defaults to the current county holder; exact context participant
`official` and its portrait card are mandatory. Binding retains the original
appointment, county and court. Missing or changed sources cannot recast a party.

An agenda can opt into `binding:{kind:'mandate_holder'|'mandate_court',officeId}`.
Holder binding excludes sovereigns and pins the first actual personal warrant.
Court binding authenticates the original incumbent and follows an actual
subordinate's warrant. Only court binding permits the dismissal operation.
Optional `waitForOffice:true` on a court binding keeps a program dormant until
its original protagonist actually becomes that court's ruler. Dormant records
use generation zero and expose only the costless stop; live binding records the
real generation. No mandate, succession or appointment is initialized on restore.

`opening_profile` criterion `mandate_transaction` names a required `storyId` and
declared `choiceId` performing one of these operations. Household source stages
must be nonrepeatable so later decisions cannot overwrite completed goal evidence.
A successful canonical
commit adds an `office:{appointmentId,action}` provenance field to that ordinary
story decision. Opening goals read it, never an acknowledgment or quote. Matching
saved decisions validate the original appointment, holder, operation and loss
response; completed goals require their source evidence. Exact current-content
restoration neither replays transactions nor pays fees again. Existing unopted
declarations and records retain their prior shapes.

The household's `travel_realm` operation takes `realmId` and `purposeId` for an
existing untargeted capitals journey. Its pure snapshot and commit delegate to
the ordinary travel owner. It does not bypass route, price, residence or personal
eligibility rules.

## Court event pack

Twenty fictional situations are available in the existing 476 and 493 campaigns.
Eligibility requires the late-antique era and years 476–550. Every event observes a current Roman civic
county holder. Eight offer canonical office or mandate reviews with unconditional
refusal; the rest use ordinary household choices. Rivals and priests are bound
participants. Audience rehearsal uses the declared Diplomacy chance.

The event ids are `service_court_petition`, `service_court_scope`,
`service_court_gates`, `service_court_accounts`, `service_court_interests`,
`service_court_favor`, `service_court_loss`, `service_court_purple`,
`service_court_pay_list`, `service_court_rival`, `service_court_senators`,
`service_court_bishop`, `service_court_watch`, `service_court_seal`,
`service_court_purser`, `service_court_purple_rumor`, `service_court_muster`,
`service_court_audience`, `service_court_succession` and `service_court_dismissed`.
These incidents do not force historical accession, territorial changes,
conversion, recognition or another principal's consent. The purple review creates
only the existing eligible claim; independent support and political resolution
remain necessary. Cancellation changes no money, people, RNG or identity counter.
Automated event resolution uses the same quote and commit or declared refusal.

The [Notitia Dignitatum](https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/source/notitiadignitatum.asp)
provides earlier institutional context. These incidents, fees, deadlines and
service terms are simulation choices, not a recovered contemporary roster.
The existing `court_office_mandates`, household, ruler-agenda and opening-goal
technology decisions remain **none**: these adapters expose their existing
transactions and observation, with no new technology eligibility.

Literal source tables use the existing householdStory, rulerAgenda, openingProfile
and event display owners. Shared review chrome uses `FB.T`; saved records contain
ids and transaction facts rather than rendered prose. The shared story/event
sheets retain ordinary cancellation, keyboard and compact navigation.

Focused coverage is `tests/e2e/specs/imperial-service-story-reviews.spec.js` and
the shared event-pack validator. The independent Lantern ordinary JSON import
uses different courts, office terms and fees through the same APIs. Device,
appearance and play acceptance remain separate from automated coverage.
