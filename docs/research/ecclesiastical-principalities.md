Ecclesiastical temporal profiles

Papal territory in 1204

The existing 1204 map supplies the temporal territory; this change neither enlarges it to every Catholic see nor treats spiritual primacy as possession. County boundaries remain the bookmark's coarse territorial abstraction. The new form binds that polity to the canonical Roman claimant and reuses existing schism and occupation behavior.

The Lateran III decree of 1179 requires two-thirds agreement for papal election and distinguishes it from other ecclesiastical procedures. The existing twoThirds law, already dated 1179 to 1273, is reused for 1204. Enclosed conclave procedure is not enabled. The earlier 1059 profile and early bookmarks retain their existing behavior; no new interpretation of disputed royal assent is assigned to them.

Source: Lateran III, decree concerning papal elections, translated in Henderson, Select Historical Documents of the Middle Ages, pages 336-337. https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/source/lat3-elect.asp

Durham reuse profile

Durham World Heritage Site describes the bishop's temporal responsibilities for England's northern frontier, the capacity to raise troops and levy taxes, and allegiance to the English king. This supports an independently configured episcopal temporal institution under a lay superior, not a sovereign rule for every bishop. Its public history uses the retrospective Prince Bishop label. It does not establish every chapter or investiture procedure for every date.

Source: Durham World Heritage Site, The Prince Bishops of Durham. https://www.durhamworldheritagesite.com/learn/history/prince-bishops

The Durham policy is exercised in an isolated acceptance fixture, not assigned to a new dated opening. A separate chapter, eligible existing local cleric and lay temporal recognition are explicit simulation approximations. Fees, reserve, service fraction and the shared bargain's support threshold are gameplay parameters. The fixture's county is not a claim that the episcopal estate covered every household or settlement in the historical county. Private property retains its owners.

The Concordat of Worms illustrates why sacred office, canonical selection and secular regalia need separate boundaries. Its German and other imperial procedures differ. It informs that separation only; it is not imported as English election law or a universal religious constitution.

Source: Concordat of Worms, 1122, translated in Henderson, pages 408-409. https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/worms1.asp

Fictional reuse

The sanctuary fixture declares its own faith, local eligibility allowing either sex, chapter and temporal profile. It has no universal lay-recognition requirement and cannot create a papal college. It demonstrates that neither Catholic identity nor a historical realm id selects the algorithm. No additional historical opening is asserted by this fixture.
