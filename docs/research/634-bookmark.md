Autumn 634 identity declarations

Scope

The current implementation stages identity data only. It registers no 634 world, court, community, start, religious office or historical relative. No existing date is modified by loading the source. A later complete world composition owns publication and the physical distribution of communities.

All three permit the ordinary eight identity consumers only within the 634 bookmark context. Their continued eligibility after the opening is a campaign convention, not evidence for later historical institutions. Head, holy-war tradition, raiding tradition and religious paths are explicitly absent; Early Islam also removes the inherited tournament tradition. Source-faith availability

The fifteen additive selectors retain the earlier regional presentation pools. Adding 634 to a bookmark selector establishes technical eligibility only. It does not establish unchanged populations, majorities, political borders or religious continuity. All other restrictions and earlier permissions must survive publication. The older Sasanian profile, Mesopotamian traditional profile and 711 caliphal profile receive no extension.

Evidence boundary

These are first-pass simulation contracts. The file makes no new claim about a named person's age, residence, family, jurisdiction or local institution. Historical background for the period is discussed by Fred M. Donner, The Early Islamic Conquests (Princeton University Press, 1981), and Walter E. Kaegi, Byzantium and the Early Islamic Conquests (Cambridge University Press, 1992). These bibliographic references supply context, not evidence for the specific mechanical settings or a claim of newly inspected passages. Exact local law, specialist institutions and communities require their own evidence before declaration.
