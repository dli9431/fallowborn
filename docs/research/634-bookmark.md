Autumn 634 identity declarations

Scope

The identity source stages its definitions without registering a 634 world, court, community, start, religious office or historical relative. No existing date is modified by loading the source. A later complete world composition owns publication and the physical distribution of communities. World sources described below also remain inert until that complete composition.

All three permit the ordinary eight identity consumers only within the 634 bookmark context. Source-faith availability

The fifteen additive selectors retain the earlier regional presentation pools. Adding 634 to a bookmark selector establishes technical eligibility only. The older Sasanian profile, Mesopotamian traditional profile and 711 caliphal profile receive no extension.

Evidence boundary

These identity definitions are first-pass simulation contracts and make no new claim about a named person's age, residence, family, jurisdiction or local institution. Historical background for the period is discussed by Fred M. Donner, The Early Islamic Conquests (Princeton University Press, 1981), and Walter E. Kaegi, Byzantium and the Early Islamic Conquests (Cambridge University Press, 1992). These bibliographic references supply context, not evidence for the specific mechanical settings or a claim of newly inspected passages. Exact local law, specialist institutions and communities require their own evidence before declaration.
