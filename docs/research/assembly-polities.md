Northern coastal households: evidence and simulation limits

Source

Marika Mägi, 2013. Corporate power structures as indicated in archaeological evidence: the case of Estonia in the Middle Iron Age and Viking Age. Fennoscandia Archaeologica 30, pages 107-125.

https://osiliana.eu/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Magi_FA_2013-Est-society.pdf

The study distinguishes the Middle Iron Age, approximately AD 450-800, from the Viking Age, approximately AD 800-1050. It interprets burial evidence and its regional variation in terms of corporate family and elite authority. Its earlier period overlaps the game's 476 opening, but this does not date a particular electoral institution to that year. Later-period evidence cannot establish the same political arrangement for the fifth century. Corporate authority is compatible with unequal influence and does not demonstrate a universal democratic franchise.

Opening interpretation

The game places a deliberately bounded community on the northern Estonian coast in the wider 476 bookmark. It uses the existing tallinn map cell as geographic coverage, not as a claim that the later city or a national Estonian state existed. The choice is a cautious simulation of local corporate authority, not an attested named polity. The opening is called Northern coastal households; Vaino and the other representatives are fictional characters.

Cultivating households, shore households and local defenders are authored gameplay interests. Their weights, eligibility ages, skill requirements, support thresholds, recall rules and public-resource limits are explicit simulation choices. No fixed term is assigned to this historical opening. The implementation makes no claim of a written constitution, annual election, universal household membership or a known fifth-century office title.

The separate Settlement federation example is wholly fictional. Its two-year mandate, service requirement, fee and different representation demonstrate configurable mechanics and provide no historical evidence for another community. Settlement remains distinct from political evidence: an inhabited landscape alone does not establish collective government.
