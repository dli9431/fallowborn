Spring 1096 opening

Status

The regional declarations below are a first pass built from the separate 1066 world, with explicit changes to its courts. They are staged data, awaiting composition into a complete opening. County populations, development, settlement slots, broad title groups and most territorial partitions remain simulation inputs inherited from that baseline. A named ruler does not establish the exact borders, household, officeholders or religious institutions of a region. Each regional replacement will supply its own evidence and remove the corresponding first-pass block.

Imperial, Italian, northern British and Balkan first pass

data/bookmark_1096_scaffold_b.js declares six separate blocks: empire_rhine (20 counties), empire_alps (19), italy_north (20), italy_south (20), britain_north_ireland (31) and balkans (22). Together they account for 132 inhabited county anchors. Their physical coordinates, terrain, development, communities and ordered settlement presentations are independent literal copies of the 1066 inhabited_v2_governments_v2 definition at 0f5f442278f91978038f181f6f9300a641f14ee5. The North Atlantic child geography is outside this opening. Copying the baseline is a mechanical simulation choice, not evidence of unchanged occupation or demographics between the two dates.

The imperial court is Henry IV, born 1050, at the abstract Frankfurt anchor. Upper Lorraine is separated from the Bouillon holdings defined elsewhere. Upper Lorraine, Bavaria and Saxony retain coarse imperial links as a first-pass constitutional reconstruction; the Welf dispute, regional opposition and actual episcopal appointments remain unresolved. Cologne, Mainz, Worms and the other imperial counties have no newly invented bishops or protective contacts. The existing Jewish communities retain their separate resident identities and numerical simulation weights.

Matilda of Canossa is seeded as a woman born 1046, chosen inside the scholarly 1045-1046 birth interval. Modena is an existing map anchor for her court, not a claim of permanent residence. It joins the inherited Tuscan bloc as a first-pass Canossan allocation; the remaining imperial counties of northern Italy retain the single imperial court. No imperial liege link is imposed on Matilda's effective opposition government, and Welf is not installed as her current co-ruler. Her exact estates, legal ties, separation and playable household require independent review. Venice has a generated incumbent and the inherited territorial mechanics as an explicit government approximation, pending its separate constitutional review.

Urban II, born approximately 1040, replaces Alexander II. His Rome anchor and the inherited papal counties, including a provisional Benevento allocation, represent a coarse temporal jurisdiction; Clement III's rival authority and the actual control of each place remain unresolved. The existing ecclesiastical_principality contract and papal_temporal_1066 profile preserve election ownership through the Catholic office. Reusing that unrestricted profile is an administration approximation, not a finding that temporal government was unchanged. No duplicate papal election, named cardinal roster or local institution is added.

Bohemond's Apulian lordships use Taranto, Bari and Brindisi, with Taranto as the provisional court anchor and birth 1054 selected within the scholarly 1051-1058 range. This is a county-scale reconstruction of his Italian possessions. It does not award all Apulia, Antioch or the later formal title of Prince of Taranto. The remaining Apulian and Calabrian bloc, including Salerno, has a generated Norman Catholic ruler at Foggia; Capua has its own generated court. Roger I replaces the earlier Sicilian emir, with birth 1030 selected within a reconstruction interval of 1028-1032 around the approximate source date. Palermo, Messina and Syracuse share this court, while their copied Muslim, Greek and Latin communities remain distinct. Palermo is a simulation court anchor, not a sourced personal residence. Sardinia and Corsica remain one provisional source-sized aggregate with a generated ruler; this does not establish a united island monarchy or resolve Pisan and local claims.

The northern English counties reference england_1096, whose court is defined in the southern British block, without another English ruler. Alba, the Isles and Man, Ulaid, Connacht, Leinster, Munster and Dublin have generated incumbents in their copied county partitions. No Malcolm III, 1066 Irish ruler or later Norwegian conquest is carried forward. Dublin's Norse Catholic court classification is a broad presentation abstraction distinct from its copied Gaelic and Norse residents. Scottish succession, Irish overlapping authority and island control remain unresolved.

Alexios I Komnenos replaces Constantine X, born 1057 as the fixed input within the approximate 1055-1057 reference range. Constantinople remains the court anchor. administrative_monarchy with the unrestricted byzantine_1066 profile retains public provincial appointment and accession mechanics; that profile's dated display name is an acknowledged first-pass approximation, not a reconstruction of the Komnenian office roster. Anatolian references use this same court rather than defining a second Alexios. Duklja and Rascia have one provisional generated Slavic Orthodox court at Kotor. The Croatian court has a generated Slavic Catholic ruler at Knin; Croatian references outside this block use that one court. The combined Serbian and Bosnian extent, coastal claims and transit rights remain unresolved. A county's color grants no negotiated access.
