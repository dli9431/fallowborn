Cashel district in 476

The wider 476 bookmark contains a small Cashel local kingship. It represents a local political community around an early centre of power, not a reconstructed kingdom of all Munster. The original 476 bookmark retains its existing footprint. Culture never automatically assigns this government to other Irish or northern communities.

Evidence and limits

Patrick, Confessio, sections 41-42 and 52-53, describes royal children, Christian converts, kings, gifts, escorts and regional judges. This supports modelling several personal and political relationships rather than equating kingship with ownership of every household. It does not establish an exact year, succession constitution, district boundary or electorate for Cashel. Patrick's chronology is itself uncertain. https://www.confessio.ie/etexts/confessio_english

The Office of Public Works visitor guide places Cashel's emergence as a centre of power in the fourth or fifth century and connects the later kingship with Eoganacht lineages. It explicitly presents the founder and Patrick baptism accounts as tradition. This is useful evidence for the location and regional association, not a contemporary register of officeholders. https://heritageireland.ie/assets/uploads/2020/03/Final-Rock-of-Cashel-English-2018_LR.pdf

Authored simulation

The ruler, ancestor and two potential successors have generated names and a fictional family graph. No named historical monarch, formal tanistry, fixed four-generation eligibility rule or universal Irish high kingship is asserted. The district's village settlement, development, Nicene community label and political extent are coarse campaign abstractions, not a census or a claim that every inhabitant shared one faith. Later monumental Cashel is not placed in the fifth-century village.

Adult kin eligibility, two local interests, influence weights, support thresholds, negotiation prices and levy/revenue fractions are gameplay choices. The model admits descent through either parent and declares age and optional sex restrictions in a profile. The first profile does not turn the absence of a known ruler into automatic male primogeniture. A separate fictional river-kin fixture changes age, sex, ordering, scopes, weights and obligations to exercise reuse without asserting another historical constitution.
