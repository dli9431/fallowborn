476 wider opening: evidence and disputed models

Source locators for the corrected wider_v2 declaration. Source ids below are bibliographic shorthand. A source can support a regional authority relationship without identifying a county border, a living incumbent or a population share. Later witnesses and geographical extrapolations are identified explicitly. The model remains a reconstruction open to historical review.

Constitutional choices

Roman civil officials exercise delegated public authority; the county appointment is a simulation unit, not a claim that all thirteen districts each had a praeses. Sasanian imperial control and hereditary provincial interests remain separate. Gulf local regna now have a Sasanian liege and keep direct possession under that superior. The Darband garrison does not confer Persian ownership of the northern rivers.

Lineage kingship represents recognized local households, best supported by Irish ruler/kin relationships and much less certain in distant uplands. Assemblies represent locally constrained authority without invented royal genealogies. The river_households_476 constituency uses cultivators, waterway households and guards for inland districts. Its weights, thresholds and levy ceilings are gameplay values. A lack of evidence for kings is not proof that an assembly elected its leaders.

Named Saragur and Oghur followings use mobile_confederation, their own conserved populations, supplies and hosts, and scoped access agreements. Their initial Don/Volga camps, host agreements and access to the Syr Darya/Sarysu are uncertain modelling choices, not attested itineraries. Local residents remain separate from the mobile coalitions; assemblies at their camps no longer substitute for mobile political life. Taman uses a distinct Bosporan coastal court. Roman protection or Hunnic tribute are plausible alternatives, but neither is silently turned into an attested 476 contract.

Name-pool discipline

The cited rulers Felethus, Giso, Gibuld, Sharhabil, Mundhir, Aswad, Khingila, Mihira, Aspar, Ardabur, Goar, Sangiban, Ezana, Ousanas and Aphilas are removed from these generated pools. Named historical actors belong in dated opening declarations, not in lists selected to stand for their peoples. Bactrian common-person forms use BAC. Other replacement pools contain constructed or broadly traditional personal forms, explicitly not a verified local fifth-century prosopography. Their dates and spellings remain a separate research limitation; removing royal names does not make every replacement attested.

Vistula, Dnieper, Indus, Horn coastal and Sahelian regional identities have explicit mechanical defaults instead of unrelated Gothic, Persian or Nubian parents. Indus names are personal compounds rather than bare deity labels. Horn coast names omit Samir and Adal. These neutral regional profiles do not assert a single known language across all their districts.

Remaining evidential limits

Exact inland constitutions, small northern and desert settlement coordinates, and most county faith shares remain low-confidence reconstructions. Volubilis is now a checked counterexample, not blanket support for western Moroccan sites. Regional settlement evidence is explicitly separated from the unproven placement of an individual site. These limits require historical acceptance; a valid data file or passing simulation test cannot supply missing evidence.
