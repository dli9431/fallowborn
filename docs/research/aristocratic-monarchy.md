Aristocratic monarchy: the 476 Sasanian profile

Evidence and abstraction

The court survey distinguishes royal kin, great families and public offices. The game models these as separate sources of eligibility, influence and jurisdiction. It does not claim that the surviving evidence establishes a numbered spring-476 voting assembly or a uniform provincial constitution. Recognition weights, majority threshold, provincial fractions, review prices and the generated opening households are simulation choices. https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/courts-and-courtiers-ii/

Peroz's reign supplies the existing royal identity and kin. The account of magnates selecting Balash after Peroz's death in 484 supports influence on accession, but supplies no predetermined event for a campaign beginning in 476. The opening keeps the declared royal family and introduces no scheduled Balash succession, Hephthalite defeat or royal deposition. https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/peroz-firuz/

Mihran and Karen

The Peroz survey describes Raham of the Mihran family supporting Peroz's accession. That establishes a relevant great-family relationship; it does not establish Raham as a researched provincial incumbent in 476. The opening therefore generates a Mihran principal and an adult relative in the Ray region. Their names and biographies are generated. Their specific public share and local jurisdiction are gameplay abstractions, not an identification of Raham or a reconstructed hereditary commission. https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/peroz-firuz/

The Karen survey places the family's traditional homeland around Nihavand and identifies later figures whose careers do not establish a named 476 principal. Hamadan is the game's coarse map scope for this regional interest. Its generated Karen household does not claim that Sukhra's later prominence belongs to the opening year or that an entire game county was privately owned by the family. https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/karin/

Provincial titles and private estates

The social and court surveys distinguish hereditary standing from office and discuss provincial and financial officials. Shahrab is used as a provincial public title, with a county-sized game jurisdiction explicitly described as an abstraction. The capital governor is generated, not a named incumbent inferred from a later list. Royal revenue officers and army commanders are generic period roles. No four-quarter military organization, universal military estate grant or sixth-century fiscal reform is imported. https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/class-system-iii/ https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/courts-and-courtiers-ii/

The Armenian survey describes the end of Arsacid kingship and the continuing role of hereditary local magnates. This supports distinguishing imperial supervision, appointed administration, religious communities and inherited local rights. The profile does not create a new Armenian royal incumbent or equate supervision with religious conversion and wholesale loss of private property. It does not schedule the later revolt. https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/armeno-iranian-relations-in-the-pre-islamic-period/

Limits of rollout

These sources support this dated court as a first consumer of the reusable mechanic. They do not establish that every settled Iranian or eastern monarchy has the same families, offices or recognition rule. The 493 court, Hephthalites and other possible consumers retain their existing forms until independently dated profiles are declared. The fictional chartered court is acceptance content rather than a historical claim.
