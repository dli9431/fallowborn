Material and bookhand refinements

Scope

Feltmaking and Regularized Bookhands add two optional national research choices to the existing catalogue. They use the current prerequisite, historical timing, effect, research and save owners. This adds no physical workshop, material stock, copying institution, office, mandatory script or military resource. Every older technology and building retains its identity and requirements.

Felt evidence and interpretation

HERODOTUS_IV_73_75 identifies Herodotus, Histories IV.73-75, George Rawlinson translation, the account of felt coverings. The passage is evidence of ancient material use. It supplies neither a production census nor a medieval national diffusion chronology. Its ethnographic account is not a rule for generated people. Source: https://classics.mit.edu/Herodotus/history.4.iv.html

RUDENKO_PAZYRYK_FELTS identifies Sergei I. Rudenko, Frozen Tombs of Siberia: The Pazyryk Burials of Iron Age Horsemen, translated by M. W. Thompson (University of California Press, 1970), its account of the felt wall hangings, saddle covers and appliqué figures recovered from the frozen Pazyryk barrows. The finds are physical evidence of skilled ancient felting in the Altai. Like the Herodotus passage, they supply no production census or medieval diffusion chronology; together the two sources bound the early evidence envelope only.

The research record's -500 to 600 evidence envelope is a rounded modeling range rather than an invention date. Regional transmission is reconstructed; every numerical adoption pair and the cost saving are simulation choices. Feltmaking has no prerequisite, costs 20 and adds -0.005 to the existing enterprise setup cost modifier. That saving combines additively with other technology savings before the existing 0.55 cost-factor floor. Basic craft, shelter and enterprise setup remain possible without the refinement. Its technology-impact decision is soft.

Bookhand evidence and interpretation

HMML_ST_GALL_116 identifies Carin Ruff's HMML School description of St. Gall 116, page 3, dated 800-835. Its Caroline hand and hierarchy of scripts illustrate specialized manuscript practice. They do not establish universal adoption in 800. Source: https://hmmlschool.org/folio/15/

CAMBRIDGE_MOORE_BEDE identifies Cambridge University Library MS Kk.5.16. The catalogue's introduction describes the eighth-century insular hand and later Caroline additions and corrections. These are distinct practices, with older efficient copying available independently of a Caroline program. Source: https://medieval.lib.cam.ac.uk/catalogue/University%20Library/MS-KK-00005-00016

MET_BOOKHANDS identifies Hannah Korn, Scripts in Development, Metropolitan Museum of Art. Its Arabic manuscript discussion and account of Greek minuscule support treating writing traditions separately. The combined 650-850 envelope summarizes different examples; neither it nor the regional schedules dates a universal script reform. Source: https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2012/byzantium-and-islam/blog/topical-essays/posts/script

Regularized Bookhands costs 50, requires Scriptoria and Classical Grammar, and adds 0.005 education within the unchanged 0.20 national cap. Consistent specialist letterforms, layout and correction are the modeled refinement. Ordinary literacy, teaching, copying and Scriptoria remain available without it. The technology-impact decision is soft. The consolidation, prices, small effect and numerical diffusion schedule are simulation choices, not measured educational outcomes.

Fresh knowledge and retained campaigns

The ordinary historical seed selects the earliest regional emergence, breaking an equal-emergence tie by widespread adoption, and keeps that complete pair. At 867 Feltmaking is complete for Latin, Byzantine, Islamic, Persianate, Slavic, Nordic, Steppe and Caucasian traditions; Baltic-Finnic and Northeast African traditions have it exposed. The default schedule also completes it. No tradition completes Regularized Bookhands at 867. It is exposed for Latin, Byzantine, Islamic, Persianate, Slavic, Caucasian and Northeast African traditions, and remains undiscovered for Nordic, Steppe, Baltic-Finnic and the default schedule.

At 1066 all ten traditions and the default schedule complete Feltmaking. Latin, Byzantine, Islamic, Persianate, Caucasian and Northeast African traditions also complete Regularized Bookhands; the other traditions and the default have it exposed. Before existing caps or authored exceptions, the corresponding new national effects are a 0.005 reduction in the enterprise cost factor and, where the bookhand refinement completes, 0.005 education. No older technology gains a new prerequisite, so the two additions do not pull extra older knowledge into those fresh sets. Explicit omissions still remove the omitted node and any dependent completion.

These are fresh-game changes. Matching saves retain their actual completed and exposed knowledge, active projects, partial progress, research reserve and property without historical reseeding. A campaign that lacks the refinements may research them through the ordinary project owner. There is no new saved root or migration.
