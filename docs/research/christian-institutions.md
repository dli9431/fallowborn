Dated Christian institutions

Evidence and representation

data/christian_rollout.js binds independent offices and service groups to explicit opening revisions after government composition. Original revisions retain their previous definitions. eastern, catholic and orthodox remain the existing primary faiths; regional office names and optional local participation distinguish institutions without converting resident communities. The 493 consumers retain their existing declarations.

Office tenure is evidence for an incumbent, not a population share, precise residence, biography or future appointment law. Off-map declarations mean that no supported map seat is assigned, including cases geographically inside the map. Their empty jurisdiction prevents county mediation. Mapped service groups use ordinary settlement anchors as simulation locations, not identified church buildings. Vacant profile groups begin without members, clergy, land or funds. Generated age, skills, portraits and household details are simulation choices.

East Syriac offices

Babowai at 476 is a reconstruction within the disputed election and death intervals in Arthur Voobus, BABOE, Encyclopaedia Iranica III/3, p. 317, election and death paragraphs: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/baboe-catholicos. Exact accession, birth and death dates are not scripted.

The 711 vacancy and later candidates are supported by Wilhelm Baum and Dietmar W. Winkler, The Church of the East (2003), appendix pp. 173-174: vacancy 700-714, Timothy I 780-823, Sergius 860-872, Sabrisho III 1064-1072 and Yahballaha II 1190-1222. Baghdad is mapped only for the 800 and 867 consumers; see the return to Baghdad discussion on p. 64. The 711 office is not placed in Baghdad. Source: https://www.assyrianlibrary.com/assyrianbooks/Religion/The%20Church%20of%20the%20East%20-%20A%20concise%20history%20-%20Wilhelm%20Baum%20and%20Dietmar%20W.%20Winkler.pdf.

Samarkand household service in 800 is a continuity reconstruction from established East Syriac presence before 762, not an exact metropolitan foundation or numerical minority. Nicholas Sims-Williams, CHRISTIANITY iii, Encyclopaedia Iranica V/5, pp. 530-534, Sogdia discussion: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/christianity-iii/. Only the revised 800 layout gains the shared city anchor; the baseline layout is preserved. Later Samarkand and Khwarazm additions remain undeclared.

Alexandrian and West Syriac offices

Alexander II in 711 is supported by History of the Patriarchs, Evetts, Patrologia Orientalis 5, chapter XVII, pp. 48-49: https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/severus_hermopolis_hist_alex_patr_03_part3.htm. Mark II in 800 is the forty-ninth patriarch, called Mark III in Evetts, Patrologia Orientalis 10, chapter XIX. Its pp. 403-410 describe Mark and Cyriacus exchanging synodical letters. These support reciprocal personal recognition and separate offices, not one universal head or a political veto. Source: https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/severus_hermopolis_hist_alex_patr_04_part4.htm.

Shenute I in 867 follows Aziz S. Atiya, YUHANNA, Coptic Encyclopedia, pp. 2356a-2356b, identifying the scribe and the 858-880 patriarchate: https://ccdl.claremont.edu/digital/api/collection/cce/id/1935/download. Christodoulos in 1066 follows History of the Patriarchs II, pp. 263-264, as excerpted by Vantini: https://www.medievalnubia.info/dev/index.php/Severus_(Sawirus). John VI in 1204 follows Dayr Anba Antuniyus, Coptic Encyclopedia, first page, specifying his 1189-1216 tenure: https://ccdl.claremont.edu/digital/api/collection/cce/id/617/download. None of these consumers supplies a researched fixed residence or monastic property.

John bar Shushan is the 1066 West Syriac candidate, with recognition in 1063/64 following a disputed earlier election. See Lucas Van Rompay, GEDSH, pp. 443-444, referenced by Syriaca person 193: https://syriaca.org/person/193. Michael II the Younger and rival recognition prevent one default West Syriac incumbent in 1204; Syriaca person 223 references the relevant GEDSH biography: https://syriaca.org/person/223. The game leaves that office unassigned. This is not a claim that no historical claimants existed. Local obedience, residence and future election procedure remain unresolved.

Armenian and Nubian consumers

Zacharias is an independently dated 867 candidate within 855-876. Tim Greenwood, Armenian Neighbours, Cambridge History of the Byzantine Empire, p. 351 and notes 69-72, discusses his correspondence and the 862 council while emphasizing disputed chronology and confessional diversity: https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/1032/3/CambridgeHistoryByzantineEmpireArmenianNeighboursGreenwood.pdf. No Dvin residence, universal Chalcedonian acceptance or new Armenian county identity is inferred.

Ibrim groups at 711, 800 and 867 are reconstructions from the dated South Church texts, DBMNT 1732 and 1734. Their ranges may extend after an opening and do not date a particular incumbent: https://www.dbmnt.pcma.uw.edu.pl/texts-general/?page=145. Dongola service at 867 is a continuity reconstruction from the earlier royal visit in History of the Patriarchs, Joseph, 830-849, Evetts, Patrologia Orientalis 10, chapter XX: https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/severus_hermopolis_hist_alex_patr_04_part4.htm. Ibrim service at 1066 uses local institutional continuity supported by Marianou's epitaph, DBMNT 77: https://www.dbmnt.pcma.uw.edu.pl/texts/77/. His Pachoras office is not converted into an Ibrim bishopric.

Dongola and Soba service at 1204 reconstruct local continuity from Abu Salih, Churches and Monasteries of Egypt, Evetts and Butler (1895), Nubian excerpts in Vantini, pp. 326-327: https://www.medievalnubia.info/dev/index.php/Abu_Salih_the_Armenian. The compilation's numerical and universal population claims are not adopted. Ibrim restoration after the 1172/73 raid remains unresolved; no new 1204 active institution is supplied there.

Latin and Greek roles

Greek groups at Constantinople in 1066 and 1204 accept already married presbyters and unmarried monastics separately, following the bounded role distinctions of Trullo (692), canons 6, 13 and 44: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3814.htm. Membership neither ordains a priest nor permits a new marriage after ordination. Episcopal continence is not approximated by an unmarried membership gate. Latin service in 1204 requires unmarried clergy and monastics, following Lateran I (1123), canon 21: https://www.papalencyclicals.net/councils/ecum09.htm. Admission does not annul existing marriages, and this consumer is not backdated to 1066.

The 1054 censures do not justify an automatic global change of communion; see the 1965 Catholic-Orthodox declaration, paragraphs 2-3: https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/en/speeches/1965/documents/hf_p-vi_spe_19651207_common-declaration.html. The 1204 conquest does not convert the Greek population; Innocent III's aftermath letter illustrates distinct political occupation and church interests: https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/1204innocent.asp. Exact opening Greek and Latin patriarchs, local obedience and marriage remedies remain undeclared. Existing papal candidates and election rules remain authoritative.

Service rules and limits

Age, skill floors, two-helper consent, two-gold supply costs, reserves, one-day service and one-time piety rewards are explicit simulation rules. Household and helper admission grants no ordination, property or public office. Dated local service and opening office correspondence have technology impact none; ordinary career requirements remain separate. Exact monastic sites, endowed services, deaconess communities, succession procedures and unsupported regional faith replacements remain unresolved. No service group is a newly endowed religious house.
