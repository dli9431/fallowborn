Evidence and limits

Cassiodorus's copying instructions in Institutions I.30, reproduced and discussed in Thomas Hodgkin's Letters of Cassiodorus (1886), introductory pp. 57-63, provide sixth-century manuscript-learning context. Material-independent copying is a reconstruction; no exact named foundation or physical library inventory is accepted. https://www.gutenberg.org/files/18590/18590-h/18590-h.htm

The Stiftsbibliothek St. Gallen catalogue for Cod. Sang. 1092 identifies an early ninth-century parchment plan with explanatory inscriptions. This is an attested plan rather than a completed-building survey. No school, hospital or library placement is derived from it. https://www.e-codices.unifr.ch/de/list/one/csg/1092

The University of Bologna's The birth of the Studium and the Commune describes gradually organized students and teaching and a traditional 1088 date. This supports distinguishing advanced teaching from a single completed corporate building. It establishes no in-game charter, exact campus, personal immunity or paper prerequisite. https://www.unibo.it/en/university/who-we-are/our-history/nine-centuries-of-history/the-birth-of-the-studium-and-the-commune

Procopius, Buildings I.2, Aubrey Stewart translation (1888), printed pp. 14-15, describes Sampson's earlier charitable hospice and rebuilding after fire. This is attested narrative evidence for care outside the later endowed-hospital model. It supplies no universal foundation year, clinical floor plan or numerical protection rate. https://www.gutenberg.org/files/65404/65404-h/65404-h.htm
