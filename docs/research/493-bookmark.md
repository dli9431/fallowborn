493 bookmark: Ravenna after the Italian war

Date: 2026-10-05. Updated: 2026-10-06. Status: implemented as a hidden review candidate in the 476 working tree. Historical acceptance, device and distribution review remain pending. The data records below distinguish sourced identities from editorial county coverage and gameplay values.

CHOSEN FRAME

Use spring 493 after Theoderic has taken Ravenna and killed Odoacer. Spring day 1 on the game's 360-day calendar would be an editorial snapshot of that completed settlement, not an assertion that its season boundary reproduces a Julian calendar date. Do not open with the earlier agreement to share Ravenna: that is a different political situation.

Procopius, Wars V.1.24-29, describes episcopal mediation, the joint settlement, Odoacer's killing and Theoderic's rule over Goths and Italians. The account supplies a narrative sequence rather than an exact daily chronology. Source: https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Procopius/Wars/5A%2A.html

ITALY AND ITS COURT

Theoderic starts at Ravenna as an established territorial king, with a Gothic Homoian elite and surviving Roman civil institutions. The independent courts of Odoacer and Romulus Augustulus and the earlier migrant host are absent. A retained Roman civic government with a distinct governing identity represents the civil administration, subject to review; Theoderic has royal rank, not western imperial rank, and his Goths do not become Nicene by declaration.

Anonymus Valesianus, latter part 55-64, describes the removal of Odoacer, Gothic acclamation, coexistence with Roman institutions, and later agreement with Anastasius. Its compressed chronology associates Zeno's death with the settlement despite the intervening years; do not use that passage to make Zeno alive in 493. Source: https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Excerpta_Valesiana/2%2A.html

Liberius holds a realm-scoped praetorian prefecture through the ordinary opening appointment contract. Cassiodorus, Variae II.16, praises his service under Odoacer, subsequent appointment by Theoderic, financial administration and the Gothic settlement. This retrospective royal letter supports a civil office and negotiated settlement, not a census or an allocation of exactly one third of every county to Gothic residents. No personal estate or settlement claim is fabricated for him. Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/18590/18590-h/18590-h.htm

Rome's senate, Ravenna's council and distinct Nicene and Homoian communities remain. Settlement proportions, development, skills, treasury amounts and army strength are editorial gameplay values. The candidate gives the Italian court the retained Dalmatian cells, Sicily and coarse Raetian/Norican coverage. These are explicit regional frontier approximations awaiting owner review, not an attested 493 county survey. Procopius's later 535 narrative establishes Gothic Sicily and Dalmatia then, but cannot prove every opening frontier. Reduced Danubian development represents disruption without dating a compulsory evacuation.

The Amal court includes Theudigotha and Ostrogotho as living daughters. Their birth years are working age estimates. The candidate creates no future Amalasuntha, forced marriage or later ruler birth. Surviving kin and ordinary household generation determine subsequent succession.

THE EASTERN COURT

Anastasius I, not Basiliscus or Zeno, holds Constantinople. Hugh Elton's scholarly account places his accession in April 491, marriage to Ariadne that May, continuing Isaurian resistance and recognition of Theoderic in 497. Recognition therefore must not be assumed as an opening 493 imperial commission. The emperor's Miaphysite sympathies and the Chalcedonian patriarchate are distinct from the beliefs of every eastern resident. Source: https://roman-emperors.sites.luc.edu/anastasi.htm

Euphemius is the Constantinopolitan patriarch for this snapshot, replacing the earlier Acacius. The Catholic Encyclopedia dates his tenure to 490-496 and describes his Chalcedonian profession and failed reconciliation with Rome. This is a confessional secondary account; use its incumbent chronology without adopting its judgments about rival churches as neutral narration. Source: https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05606a.htm

ROME AND RELIGIOUS AUTHORITY

Gelasius I replaces Simplicius at Rome. His letter to Anastasius in 494 distinguishes priestly authority and royal power and provides a near-contemporary basis for contested jurisdiction. It is later evidence for the relationship, not a dispatch already written at the opening date. It must not activate medieval papal elections, crusades or excommunication systems merely because the Roman bishop exists. Source: https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/gelasius1.asp

Independent institution declarations name Gelasius at Rome, Euphemius at Constantinople, Eugenius at Carthage and Epiphanius at Ticinum. Every other retained see generates a representative of its declared local community; an unverified name or vacancy from 476 is not carried forward. Favianis has no living Severinus declaration. Institution identities remain stable, while representatives and agreements belong to the saved campaign.

Gelasius was elected in 492; his birth year is unknown. Epiphanius was bishop of Pavia in this period, although secondary chronologies differ over his death. The declared births of these bishops and Euphemius, and all their skills, are working gameplay estimates. Sources: https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06406a.htm and https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/santo-epifanio_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/

GAUL AND IBERIA

Clovis rules the former Soissons domain as well as the retained Salian cells; his court uses the Soissons site in the stable laon map cell rather than asserting Paris as his capital in 493. Alaric II rules from Toulouse. Gundobad holds the senior Burgundian court, while Godegisel has a separate Genevan court with Geneva and Chambery. That partition is a coarse gameplay frontier. Gregory of Tours, Histories II.27-37, supplies Syagrius's fall, Burgundian rivalry and Alaric's later defeat, not a county survey. A pagan Frankish opening awaits review of the disputed baptism chronology. Source: https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/basis/gregory-hist.asp

Theuderic and Sigismund are declared sons in their respective royal courts. Their starting ages, and the birth years of Clovis, Alaric, Gundobad and Godegisel, are working chronological approximations. No conversion or death is prescribed at a later historical date. The Suevic, Rhenish Frankish, Alamannic, Armorican and Gepid rulers are explicitly generated editorial identities.

AFRICA AND OTHER REGIONS

Gunthamund rules the Vandals at Carthage. Procopius, Wars III.8, identifies him as Gento's son and records Thrasamund's subsequent accession under the dynasty's seniority arrangement. The compact court retains deceased Gaiseric, Gento and Huneric as ancestry, with living Thrasamund and Hilderic. Birth years are approximate working ages. Thrasamund is the opening seniority preference, not a scheduled replacement in 496. Later Sicilian concessions and Amalafrida's marriage are not opening grants. Source: https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Procopius/Wars/3C%2A.html

Eugenius returned under Gunthamund and is the opening bishop of Carthage, replacing the earlier vacancy. His declared birth is an editorial age estimate. Sources: https://jerihna.sourceschretiennes.org/en/authors/eugenius-episcopus-carthaginensis and https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05602c.htm

The African coastal, Aures, Altava and Tingitanian cells are coarse regional coverage. Moorish rulers are generated identities; the candidate does not project a later named Masuna or Cabaon backward into 493. Sardinia and Corsica remain Vandal. The geography supplies no playable Persia, Syria or Egypt. Alexandria, Antioch and Jerusalem retain off-map religious offices with generated contemporary representatives.

STARTS AND CONDITIONAL HISTORY

Four date-scoped openings offer a free farming household, an artisan's apprentice, a Nicene church household at Rome and a Roman curialis at Ravenna. Their introductions and family choices have no dependency on Orestes, the earlier pay dispute or western_court_476. The existing late antique vocabulary, occupations, wardrobes and technology apply.

Six independent campaign instances open opportunities on the upper Rhine (496-506), against the senior Burgundian court (500-503), toward Toulouse (507-511), at Sirmium (505-510), against Carthage (533-535) and toward Ravenna (535-540). These are editorial opportunity windows. Cassiodorus's later diplomatic letters and Gregory's account support the Frankish contests; Procopius supplies the later eastern expeditions. Each requires current court and objective possession, waits for ordinary war availability and records actual conquest, failure, diversion or interruption. The Ravenna expedition additionally requires an actual eastern African foothold. No calendar entry awards territory, invents troops, baptizes a ruler or kills a person.

Campaigns belong to surviving courts rather than requiring the original ruler to survive for decades. Once an opportunity starts, the shared campaign lifecycle stamps its actual ruler and interrupts on authority loss. Imperial recognition in 497 is contextual evidence, not an automatically issued opening or future commission; ordinary offices and diplomacy remain authoritative.
