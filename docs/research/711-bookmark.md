Spring 711 historical research and authoring decisions

Research date: 2026-10-05. The 476 branch makes this bookmark selectable for ordinary play and review. Its historical reconstruction remains incomplete. The opening is Spring day 1 in the game's seasonal calendar, before the main Iberian crossing and summer battle. It is not an asserted conversion to a precise Gregorian date. Evidence supports the principal courts; many county boundaries, ages and peripheral groupings remain game-scale approximations.

Hispania and the crossing

Roderic's accession was contested, and the conquest traditions disagree about the battle, its location and his death. The Cambridge History of Islam describes Tariq's spring 711 landing, the July battle and subsequent advance, while explicitly treating Roderic's death as uncertain. The candidate begins before a Muslim beachhead and uses separate Toledo and northeastern Gothic courts. Malaga is the shared grid's landing-region proxy. No script guarantees a death or conquest.

Achila's northeastern opposition is supported by narrative, regnal and numismatic evidence, but its precise frontier is uncertain. The rival court owns Catalonia, the middle Ebro and Septimania in this candidate; the remaining Hispania allocation to Roderic is an abstraction of claimed authority, not proof of uniform local obedience. Hispano-Roman and Gothic identities coexist within Latin Christianity, with Jewish communities retained as minority cells. Shares are game estimates and the communities make no collective claim about collaboration in the invasion.

Later northern resistance and Gaul

Pelayo and Asturias belong to the post-conquest history. Their later narratives need critical handling rather than an already-crowned northern king in 711. The opening leaves the northern uplands under the Gothic domain. A reusable conditional foundation now requires actual Umayyad possession of Toledo and Catholic community continuity. A surviving Gothic-held remnant can form Pelayo's court at peace; conquered uplands must win independence through an ordinary armed revolt. Neither the calendar nor a foundation record wins the revolt. Using the loss of Toledo as a collapse predicate and allowing a peaceful northern remnant are explicit game-scale approximations.

Cambridge University Press, Treacherous Foundations, introduction and its discussion of later Pelayo traditions: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/treacherous-foundations/introduction/15DF4B0750E42B82E1D7EC0EA5C2E00C

The Principality of Asturias tourism account gives the conventional sequence of a resistance leadership in 718 and Covadonga in 722, with the centre at Cangas de Onis. This supports keeping the northern polity out of the pre-conquest opening; it does not settle the later narrative's disputed details. The foundation's 718-730 window, three-year revolt commitment, one shared upland county, royal rank, birth year 685, skills and generated family are gameplay authoring decisions rather than reconstructed personal or administrative records. The county keeps its neutral upland presentation instead of presenting the later foundation of Oviedo as Pelayo's city.

The conventional post-conquest sequence includes Narbonne and Septimania from 719, Toulouse in 721 and the later campaign associated with Tours in 732. The candidate's rail windows permit alternate outcomes and are not recreations of those battles. Its 725 Aquitanian opportunity is deliberately later than Toulouse's original attack, allowing ordinary campaigns to determine a foothold first. It therefore requires owner acceptance as a gameplay approximation.

The Metropolitan Museum's Iberian chronology above and Cambridge's Iberian chapter provide the conquest framework; the individual campaign windows are authoring choices, not derived dates for guaranteed events.

Umayyad sovereign and provincial courts

Al-Walid I ruled in 705-715. Damascus was the Umayyad capital, and the empire was diverse rather than uniformly Arabic-speaking or Muslim. The opening declares Damascus above provincial courts for Musa at Kairouan, Tariq at Tangier, Qurra in Egypt, al-Hajjaj in Iraq and Qutayba in Khurasan. The nested liege model distinguishes sovereignty from local holders but is a coarse representation of governorships. Provincial rulers' skills and uncertain ages are game estimates. No future Abbasid dynasty is imported from 867.

The Metropolitan Museum, The Art of the Umayyad Period (661-750): https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-art-of-the-umayyad-period-661-750

The Sunni parent is a shared game-doctrine abstraction. umayyad_sunni_711 expresses allegiance to a separate caliphal institution and claims Damascus or the combined holy cities; it is not an assertion that a later doctrinal school existed in 711. The office uses the existing caliphal-claim strategy. Arab Muslim ruling courts remain distinct from Christian, Coptic, Berber, Jewish and Zoroastrian residents. Community ratios are illustrative, not census evidence.

Francia, Britain and Ireland

Childebert III remains the Frankish king at this early-spring opening, with Pippin of Herstal's practical dominance recorded as an unmodeled office relationship. The date does not substitute Charles Martel or a Carolingian kingdom. Odo's Aquitaine, a Vasconian dependency, Breton principalities and Radbod's Frisia are distinct courts. Alamannia and Bavaria use nominal Frankish lieges; these relationships and Odo's opening chronology need review.

The Metropolitan Museum, List of Rulers of Europe, gives Childebert III's reign as 694-711 and the British rulers used here: https://www.metmuseum.org/ja/toah/hd/euru/hd_euru.htm

The opening retains Ine in Wessex, Wihtred in Kent, Ceolred in Mercia and Osred I in Northumbria. Osred is a minor, not made adult for convenience. Dumnonia and the Welsh, Pictish, Alt Clut, Dal Riata and Irish communities are coarse political aggregates. Pictish presentation borrows the existing Gaelic culture; this does not assert that Pictish identity was Gaelic. Unnamed local courts use ordinary generated rulers rather than invented historical people. Northern Ulster and the Ui Neill territories are not differentiated at county resolution and need further research.

Italy, eastern empire and Bulgaria

Aripert II, not Liutprand, is the Lombard king in spring 711. Faroald II governs Spoleto; Romuald II governs Benevento. Both duchies use nominal Lombard lieges, a simplified representation of their autonomy. Ravenna remains eastern, as do Rome, the lagoon, southern coastal districts and the islands assigned to the eastern court. Parma, Modena and Bologna are Lombard in the candidate, with Ravenna and Ancona retained in the exarchate. Several border cells require regional review.

Iran, Transoxiana and the eastern frontier

Qutayba's operations precede the conquest of Samarkand in 712. Bukhara is assigned to the Khurasanian provincial court while Samarkand, Ustrushana and Chach remain a Sogdian aggregate. Iranian residents retain substantial Zoroastrian populations under Muslim courts. Tabaristan and the Caspian districts form an independent Zoroastrian court. Kabul/Zabul and Sindh remain frontier kingdoms rather than imported Saffarid or Habbarid states.

No exact victory or conversion is pre-scripted.

Outer-map uncertainty

The northern forest, Dnieper, Vistula, Baltic, Finnic, Scandinavian and Volga regions use explicitly named communities or principalities rather than later Rus, Polish, Norwegian or Volga Bulgar kingdoms and imported 867 rulers. Their aggregate sovereigns and capitals are gameplay anchors, not historically documented unitary states. The Khazar court is Tengrist here; a settled Jewish royal court is not assumed at this date. Abkhazia, Caucasian Iberia, Armenia and the Alans use simplified frontier relationships which remain under review.

The western Berber uplands remain outside the caliphal provincial hierarchy, using the existing Amazigh traditional-faith definition. Makuria, Alodia and the Aksumite highlands are Christian courts with generated incumbents. Their exact boundaries, the extent of western Muslim rule and the mixed Maghrebi community ratios have not been established from county-level evidence. The Balearics use a Byzantine assignment as a disputed island-control approximation. These cells require regional research before public acceptance.

Settlement and constitutional cautions

Many modern county identifiers refer to later city foundations. The bookmark keeps saved identifiers stable while presenting earlier settlements or neutral regional names. The baghdad cell presents al-Mada'in, mansura presents Brahmanabad, fes presents Volubilis, murcia presents Orihuela, santiago presents Iria Flavia and oviedo presents the Asturian uplands. Urbanity and district coordinates are authoring estimates, with one head site per inhabited cell. A seed point is not archaeological proof of a town.

The d_711_, k_711_ and e_711_ tables are gameplay promotion regions. They do not imply that later medieval duchies, kingdoms, sovereign borders or imperial titles existed across the full map in 711. Realms remain separate from those title groups. All courts use the existing territorial form; Visigothic election remains an explicit dynastic-succession gap. New behavior belongs in registered platform strategies rather than bookmark selectors.

Review disposition

The bookmark is registered during ordinary boot on the 476 branch. Conditional Asturian foundation is implemented through the shared polity_foundation type; its loss-of-Toledo predicate, remnant route and ordinary revolt route still need historical and natural-play acceptance. Requirement checks, regional historical acceptance, ordinary household natural-play review and device/distribution review remain owner review tasks. Elective Visigothic succession, provincial and papal arrangements, frontier faith progression and outer-map aggregates are recorded approximations. The implementation must not be described as an accepted full historical start while those gaps remain.
